# Banff Advisors Terms Glossary

## Core Business Model Terms

### **Executive Client / Private Advisor Client**
- Individual executives who are Banff clients (CEOs, CPOs, etc.)
- Pay for services like career advisory, network building, and placement support
- Typical contract: 1-4 years
- Target: 8-10 new clients/month

### **B4B (Banff for Business) / Enterprise Client**
- Private equity firms, venture capital firms, search firms
- Pay Banff to access executive talent and intelligence
- Interact primarily through Contender platform

---

## Key Service Activities

### **P2P (Peer-to-Peer)**
- **What it is:** Introducing one Banff executive client to another executive in the Banff network
- **Purpose:** Network building, knowledge sharing, mutual benefit
- **Pain Point:** Completely manual tracking - no record of who was introduced to whom, when follow-up is needed, or what the outcome was
- **Triggered by:** Advisor check-in calls where they identify networking needs

### **Trending / Trending Campaign**
- **What it is:** A 2-month campaign where Banff sends an executive client's profile to their B4B partners (private equity firms, venture capital firms, search firms)
- **Purpose:** Create visibility for the executive in the market, generate opportunities
- **Process:** Client profiles are "whispered out" through Contender platform to relevant firms
- **Pain Point:** Labor-intensive, requires ensuring profile data is current and accurate
- **Timing:** Typically happens in first 2 months for new clients, then can be repeated later

### **Narrative Edits**
- **What it is:** Creating/updating executive career narratives (resume, LinkedIn, bio)
- **Where it lives:** Google Drive (separate folder per client)
- **Process:** 2-3 rounds of back-and-forth editing between client ops and client
- **Pain Point:** Manual coordination, scattered across Google Drive

### **Kickoff Call**
- **What it is:** 60-90 minute deep-dive call with new client
- **Participants:** Advisor + Client Ops + Client
- **Purpose:** Full career history, goals, what they're trying to achieve
- **Timing:** Happens within first few days after contract signing

### **Target Mapping**
- **What it is:** Mapping out companies/roles of interest for the client
- **Triggered by:** Quarterly check-in calls based on client needs

## Technology & Systems Terms

### **Contender**
- Banff's proprietary/in-house platform
- Functions like a "LinkedIn for executives" that Banff controls
- Where they manage executive profiles and "whisper" them to B4B partners
- **Pain Point:** Doesn't communicate well with other tools, no API

### **HubSpot**
- CRM system
- Tracks emails automatically
- Contract/billing information
- Originally chosen because: free, tracks emails, had calendar link

### **Notion**
- Where most client notes, meeting summaries, and activity tracking lives
- Client ops spends most time here
- Syncs with HubSpot via Whale Sync

### **Retool**
- Workflow automation/dashboard tool
- Used to pull data from multiple sources (HubSpot, Notion, Contender)
- Testing ground for workflows and reporting
- Client pathway management dashboard lives here

### **Whale Sync**
- Automation tool that syncs HubSpot and Notion
- Automatically creates Notion pages when HubSpot records are updated

### **TSQ**
- External bookkeeping/finance partner
- Handles contracts via DocuSign
- 24-hour turnaround on contract generation

## Common Pain Points Referenced

### **Manual Tracking**
- Introductions, feedback, impact all tracked manually
- No automated follow-up reminders
- Tasks buried in email

### **Data Entry Across 4+ Systems**
- New client setup requires manual entry in: HubSpot → Notion → Contender → Google Drive
- Same information entered multiple times

### **Profile Currency**
- Executives update their LinkedIn, but Contender profiles go stale
- Risk of sending outdated information to partners ("we look like idiots")

### **Advisor Inconsistency**
- Each advisor has their own workflow
- No standardization on how notes are taken or where they're stored
- Some use email, some use Slack, some use Notion differently
